\section{Convolutions}

The convolution product of two functions $f$ and $g$ is defined by the
followingq integral:
%
\begin{equation}
  (f\mathop{*}g)(t) = \int_{t' = -\infty}^{\infty} f(t') g(t-t') \dd t'
  \label{eq:convolution}
\end{equation}
%
This definition is of course subject to the definition domain of the
functions.

\paragraph{Conservation} The integral of a convolution product is the
product of the integrals:

\begin{equation}
  \int_{t = -\infty}^{\infty}(f\mathop{*}g)(t)\dd t
  = \int_{t = -\infty}^{\infty}\int_{t' = -\infty}^{\infty} f(t')
  g(t-t') \dd t'\dd t
\end{equation}
%
Swapping the order of integration and substituting $u = t-t'$ gives:
\begin{equation}
  \int_{t = -\infty}^{\infty}(f\mathop{*}g)(t)\dd t
  = \int_{t' = -\infty}^{\infty} f(t') \dd t'
   \times \int_{u = -\infty}^{\infty}
   g(u) \dd u
\end{equation}
%
Hence, convolving by a function of integral 1 should preserve the
integral. This is the basis for a test in
\texttt{operations/convolve.cmds}, in which a very jaggy function is
convolved by an increasingly stiff exponential. The results are shown
below:

\begin{testresult}
  \begin{center}
    \testtabular{test=convolve-conservation}{Samples|Stiffness|Residual}{samples,time@num|value@num}
  \end{center}
  \caption{Results of the tests of conserving the integral while
    convolving a jaggy function with a stiff exponential in

\end{testresult}


\subsection{Convolution of gaussians}

Let's look at the convolution product of two gaussians, with for
instance:
%
\begin{align}
  f(t) &= \exp \pa{-a\,t^2} &
  g(t) &= \exp \pa{-b\,t^2}
\end{align}
%
Then,
\begin{align}
  (f\mathop{*}g)(t) &=
  \int_{t' = -\infty}^{\infty}
  \exp \pa{-a\,t'^2}\exp \pa{-b\,[t-t']^2} \dd t'\\[3mm]
  &= 
  \int_{t' = -\infty}^{\infty}
  \exp \pab{-\pa{a+b}\,t'^2 + 2b\,t\,t' - b\,t^2} \dd t'\\[3mm]
  &= 
  \int_{t' = -\infty}^{\infty}
  \exp
  \pa{-\frac{1}{a+b}
    \pab{\pa{(a+b)t'}^2 - 2b\,t\,\pa{a+b}t' + \pa{b t}^2}
    - bt^2 + \frac{b^2t^2}{a+b}
  } \dd t'\\[3mm]
  &=
  \exp -\frac{a\,b\,t^2}{a+b} \times
  \int_{t' = -\infty}^{\infty}
  \exp
  \pa{-\frac{\pa{(a+b)t' - bt}^2}{a+b}}
  \dd t'\\[3mm]
  &= \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a+b}} \times 
  \exp \pa{-\frac{a\,b\,t^2}{a+b}}
\end{align}

These formula are the basis of a part of the tests in
\texttt{operations/convolve.cmds}. The results are shown below:

\section{Laplace transforms}

Let's consider the Laplace transform, defined by:
%
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{f}(s) = \int_{t = 0}^{\infty} f(t) \exp \pa{-st} \dd t
  \label{eq:laplace:def}
\end{equation}
%
What is implemented in \QSoas{} is the reverse Laplace transform,
i.e. taking a function in the Laplace domain and turning back into the
time domain.

The file \texttt{operations/laplace.cmds} contains a series of test of
reverse laplace transforms. This table lists the difference
(\texttt{0dev}) between the numerical reverse and the analytical
reverse in the tests in the range 0 to 10. The results are shown
below:

\begin{testresult}
  \begin{center}
    \testtabular{test=reverse-laplace}{Transform|Reverse|Precision}{function@texttt,reverse@texttt|value@num}
  \end{center}
  \caption{Results of the \texttt{operations/laplace.cmds} test,
    showing the accuracy of the reverse Laplace transforms}
\end{testresult}


The Laplace transforms has many properties.  The Laplace transform of
a derivative is given by:
%
\begin{equation}
  \int_{t = 0}^{\infty} \ddt{f}(t) \exp \pa{-st} \dd t
  = [f(t)\exp\pa{-st}]_{t = 0}^{t = \infty}
  + s \int_{t = 0}^{\infty} f(t) \exp \pa{-st}\dd t
  = s\tilde{f} - f(t = 0)
  \label{eq:laplace:ddt}
\end{equation}



\subsection{Simple kinetic models}

As a test for the reverse Laplace transform, let's consider the series
of irreversible reactions:
%
\begin{equation}
  \ce{C_{i} ->[k_i] C_{i+1}}
\end{equation}
%
in which the initial condition is $C_0 = 1$ and $C_{i>0} = 0$. The
differential equations governing the concentrations are:
%
\begin{align}
  \ddt{C_0} &= -k_0 C_0 &
  \ddt{C_i} &= k_{i-1} C_{i-1} - k_{i} C_{i} 
\end{align}
%
Taking the Laplace transform of the above equations (with the initial
conditions) and using equation~\eqref{eq:laplace:ddt} yields:
%
\begin{align}
  s \tilde{C}_0 - 1 &= -k_0 \tilde{C}_0 &
  s \tilde{C}_i = k_{i-1} \tilde{C}_{i-1} - k_{i} \tilde{C}_{i} 
\end{align}
%
In other words, the $\tilde{C}_i$ are defined by the following
recurrence:
%
\begin{align}
  \tilde{C}_0 &= \frac{1}{k_0 + s} &
  \tilde{C}_i &= \frac{k_{i-1} \tilde{C}_{i-1}}{k_i + s}
\end{align}
%
Thus:
\begin{align}
  \tilde{C}_0 &= \frac{1}{k_0 + s} & 
  \tilde{C}_1 &= \frac{k_{0}}{\pa{k_0 + s}\pa{k_1 + s}} &
  \tilde{C}_2 &= \frac{k_{0}\,k_1}{\pa{k_0 + s}\pa{k_1 + s}\pa{k_2 + s}}
\end{align}

These relationships are the base for the tests in
\texttt{operations/irreversible-steps.cmds}, which results are shown
below:
